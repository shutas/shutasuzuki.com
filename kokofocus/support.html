Support

kokofocus — Point and draw on your screen

Requires macOS 26 or later. This guide covers version 1.2.2, available on the Mac App Store.

The whole app in one line: hold Control, press and hold the mouse button, flick toward the tool you want, and let go.

Getting started

kokofocus normally lives in your menu bar. Its six-circle icon fills in when the app is on. The menu shows only Turn On kokofocus and its shortcut hint while the app is off. A Dock icon appears during single-window setup or a presentation.

Open the tool ring. Hold Control, then press and hold the mouse button. A circular menu appears at your cursor.

Choose a tool. Flick toward it and release. Release in the middle to cancel.

Draw. Select Arrow, Line, Rectangle, or Ellipse, then drag. Pointer and Laser let clicks pass through to the app underneath.

Return to your mouse. Press Esc to switch to Pointer, keeping your annotations and window share. Press Esc again from Pointer to turn kokofocus off and clear the annotations.

Turn it on or off. Double-tap Control, or use the menu bar.

The outer ring has Pointer, Laser, Arrow, Line, Rectangle, and Ellipse. The inner ring has Undo, Redo, and Clear. Actions leave the tool unchanged, and clearing is undoable. Reopen the visual Tutorial from the menu at any time.

Settings

General controls the menu gesture, double-tap shortcut, hold length, and automatic update checks.

Appearance contains the menu background, color, laser size, stroke weight, shape fills, automatic fading, and whether controls appear in screen captures. Choose Light, Dark, Liquid Glass, or Transparent with a live preview. Liquid Glass is the default; saved choices are preserved.

Shortcuts lets you assign a key or combination to each tool. The defaults are Control-P (Pointer), Control-L (Laser), Control-A (Arrow), Control-I (Line), Control-R (Rectangle), and Control-E (Ellipse). Shortcuts work while kokofocus is on and pause during its settings, tutorial, and sharing setup.

Use the × beside a tool to clear its shortcut, or Reset to Default Settings to restore all six. This changes shortcuts only. Assignments without Command or Control show a warning about typing conflicts, and unavailable macOS combinations remain saved with a warning.

Sharing your screen

Share your whole display and your annotations appear automatically. Nothing else to configure.

Annotate a Single Window (beta)

Choose Microsoft Teams, Webex, or Zoom for a guide tailored to your meeting app. Each uses short instructions and an animation. Finish the step on screen, then click the button to continue.

Webex

  1. Stop sharing first. End any active screen or window share in Webex. In kokofocus’s menu, choose Annotate a Single Window (beta) → Webex.
  2. Enable the Mac picker. In Webex, open Settings → Sharing Content and turn on Use the Mac system screen sharing picker.
  3. If you changed the setting, restart Webex. Fully quit and reopen Webex, then rejoin your meeting.
  4. Choose your app window in kokofocus. Click Choose a Window…, then select the original app window you want to annotate in the macOS picker.
  5. Share that same window in Webex. Click Continue to Sharing, then click Share in Webex. When the Mac picker appears, click the same window on your desktop that you just chose in kokofocus.

Your pointer and drawings appear in the shared window. Keep working in your original app.

Microsoft Teams and Zoom

  1. Stop sharing first. End any active screen or window share in your meeting app. In kokofocus’s menu, choose Annotate a Single Window (beta), then Microsoft Teams or Zoom.
  2. Choose your app window in kokofocus. Click Choose a Window…, then select the original app window in the macOS picker.
  3. Share the annotated window. Confirm macOS’s sharing offer when available. Otherwise, click Continue to Sharing, open Share in your meeting app, and select kokofocus — Share This Window.

While you’re sharing

When macOS reports that sharing has started, kokofocus returns you to the original app so you can annotate and present. When macOS reports that sharing has ended, kokofocus closes the window share and stays on with your selected tool. You can also choose Stop Window Share (beta) from the menu; use this if your meeting app does not report when sharing stops.

Single-window annotation includes video only. Annotations keep their size and position from the window’s top-left when you resize it; they do not follow scrolling or changes in page layout.

This feature remains in beta, and behavior can vary by meeting app. If you have trouble, sharing your whole display also includes your annotations.

The tool menu and tool indicator are excluded from captures by default. You can include them from Settings → Appearance when recording a tutorial.

Updates

Automatic checks are on by default. kokofocus checks Apple’s catalog when due at launch, daily while open, and after waking. If a check fails, it retries after an hour. Turn this off in Settings → General, or use Check for Updates whenever you want.

A compatible new release adds an Update Available menu item. If you allow macOS notifications, you also receive one quiet notification per release. Click it to read the release details, then choose Update in App Store. The App Store installs the update; kokofocus does not download or install it itself.

Common questions

I selected Rectangle and now I cannot click my slides

Drawing tools take the mouse so you can draw without holding a key. Press Esc once, or select Pointer, to return clicks to your app. The menu bar stays reachable.

Control-click stopped working

The menu modifier sends clicks to kokofocus while held. Choose another key in Settings → General → Hold to open the menu if you need Control-click. Fn has no click gesture to replace.

Double-tapping Control conflicts with another app

Turn off double-tap in Settings → General. Tool shortcuts can be changed individually in Settings → Shortcuts; the app warns about assignments that may interfere with typing.

Why is the normal mouse cursor visible with Laser?

Laser preserves slide clicks and keyboard control in your presentation app. The native cursor remains visible in this mode.

Can shapes disappear on their own?

Yes. Turn on Settings → Appearance → Fade shapes automatically and choose a duration from 2 to 30 seconds.

Does it need permissions or a connection?

Display annotation needs no Accessibility or Input Monitoring permission. Window Share uses the macOS picker to authorize one source for the session. Update notifications require your permission; update checks contact Apple’s public catalog. No account or tracking is used. See the privacy policy.

Resetting kokofocus

Your preferences live in the app’s own sandbox container. To reset them without uninstalling, quit kokofocus and delete:

~/Library/Containers/com.shutas.kokofocus
