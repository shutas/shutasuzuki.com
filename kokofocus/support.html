Support

kokofocus — Point and draw on your screen

Requires macOS 26 or later

The whole app in one line: hold Control, press and hold the mouse button, flick toward the tool you want, and let go.

Getting started

kokofocus lives in your menu bar. Click the ring icon and choose Turn On kokofocus. The icon fills in when it is on. In version 1.1, a visual Tutorial opens each time you turn it on until you select Don’t show again. You can reopen Tutorial from the menu.

Open the tool ring. Hold Control, then press and hold the mouse button. A ring appears at your cursor.

Choose a tool. Flick toward it and release. Release in the middle to cancel and change nothing.

Turn it on or off. Double-tap Control, or use the menu bar.

Return to your app. In version 1.1, press Esc to return to Pointer, keeping your annotations. Press it again from Pointer to turn kokofocus off and clear them. Version 1.0 turns off on the first press.

The ring has six tools on the outside — Pointer, Laser, Arrow, Line, Rectangle and Ellipse — and three actions on the inside: Undo, Redo and Clear. Actions fire and leave your tool alone. Clearing can be undone, so the trash is safe to use.

Sharing your screen

Share your whole display and your annotations appear automatically to everyone on the call. Nothing to configure.

Share one application window — version 1.1

1. Choose. Open Window Share (beta)… from kokofocus’s menu bar and choose your source in the macOS window picker.

2. Share. In your meeting app, select kokofocus — Share This Window. Ask a participant to confirm the blue check screen fills the shared area.

3. Present. Click Start Presenting in kokofocus. Keep working and drawing over the original app window.

Share the kokofocus presentation window, rather than the original app window, to include annotations. A temporary Dock icon helps meeting apps find it. Window Share includes video only. When you resize the source, annotations keep their size and distance from its top-left; they do not follow page layout changes or scrolling.

Using Webex? Enable Use the Mac system screen sharing picker in Webex’s sharing settings. If the picker closes without starting a share, fully quit and reopen Webex, then rejoin your meeting. Its default capture mode can show a large white surround. We have verified the native-picker path with a remote participant; other meeting clients have not had equivalent remote validation.

Version 1.0 supports display sharing only. The tool ring and indicator are excluded from capture by default; you can include them in Settings.

Tool shortcuts — version 1.1

Open Settings → Shortcuts, click a tool’s shortcut, and press a key or combination. Defaults are Control-P for Pointer, Control-L for Laser, Control-A for Arrow, Control-I for Line, Control-R for Rectangle, and Control-E for Ellipse.

Shortcuts work while kokofocus is on and interaction is active. They pause while using Settings, Tutorial, or sharing setup. Assignments without Command or Control are allowed, with a red warning about possible conflicts. If macOS cannot activate an assignment, it stays saved and a warning explains the problem. Conflicts with kokofocus’s own shortcuts are blocked.

Use the × beside a tool to clear its shortcut, or Reset to Default Settings to restore all six. This reset changes tool shortcuts only.

Updates — version 1.1

Choose Check for Updates in the menu bar. If an update is available, open the Mac App Store from the result to install it. Version 1.0 can be updated directly from the App Store.

Common questions

I selected Rectangle and now I cannot click anything

A drawing tool takes the mouse so it can see your drag. Switch back to Pointer from the tool ring or menu bar. In version 1.1, pressing Esc also returns to Pointer and keeps your annotations.

Control-click stopped working

While you hold the summoning key, clicks go to kokofocus rather than the app underneath — so that key’s own click gesture is unavailable, and Control-click is secondary click. If you rely on it, choose a different key in Settings → General → Hold to open the menu. Fn has no click gesture of its own and avoids the conflict entirely.

Double-tapping Control does nothing, or does it by accident

Two quick taps of Control, with nothing else held, toggle kokofocus. Each tap must be brief and the pair close together. If another app uses the same gesture, turn it off in Settings → General.

Nothing happens when I drag

Check that kokofocus is on — the menu bar icon is filled when it is — and that a drawing tool is selected rather than Pointer or Laser. Those two deliberately pass every click through to the app underneath.

Can I make my shapes disappear on their own?

Yes. Settings → Tools → Fade shapes automatically, with a duration from 2 to 30 seconds. Shapes that fade this way do not use an undo step.

The tool ring does not show up in my screenshots

That is deliberate — it is excluded from every screenshot, recording and screen share so your audience only sees the result. To capture it for a tutorial, turn on Settings → Tools → Show kokofocus’s controls in screen captures, then turn it off again afterwards. Note that it applies to live screen sharing too, so people on a call will see it as well.

Does it need any permissions?

No Accessibility or Input Monitoring permission is needed. Optional Window Share in version 1.1 asks you to authorize a source through the macOS picker; it does not request blanket Screen Recording access. Check for Updates contacts Apple when you request it. See the privacy policy.

Resetting kokofocus

Your preferences live in the app’s sandbox container, which macOS may retain after uninstalling. To reset all preferences, quit kokofocus and remove:

~/Library/Containers/com.shutas.kokofocus
