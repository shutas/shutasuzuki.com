Support

kokofocus — Point and draw on your screen

Requires macOS 26 or later. This guide covers version 1.3.2, available on the Mac App Store.

The whole app in one line: hold Control, press and hold the mouse button, flick toward the tool you want, and let go.

Getting started

kokofocus normally lives in your menu bar. Its six-circle icon fills in when the app is on. The menu shows only Turn On kokofocus and its shortcut hint while the app is off. A Dock icon appears during single-window setup or a presentation. At launch, only Tutorial opens automatically if you have not chosen “Don’t show again”; Settings and single-window setup open when you choose them.

Open the tool ring. Hold Control, then press and hold the mouse button. A circular menu appears at your cursor.

Choose a tool. Flick toward it and release. Release in the middle to cancel.

Draw. Select Pen, Highlighter, Arrow, Line, Rectangle, or Ellipse, then hold the left mouse button and drag. Pointer lets you click the app underneath. Laser consumes clicks and shows a ripple; press Esc for Pointer when you want to interact with your content.

Return to your mouse. Press Esc to switch to Pointer, keeping your annotations and window share. Press Esc again from Pointer to turn kokofocus off and clear the annotations.

Turn it on or off. Double-tap Control, or use the menu bar.

The disk starts with Pointer, Laser, Arrow, Line, Rectangle, and Ellipse. In Settings → General, drag tools into its five editable positions; Pointer always stays at the top. Hover over a configured tool and click its × to remove it, or drag between positions to swap. Tools already on the disk are dimmed in the available list. The inner ring has Undo, Redo, and Clear. Clearing is undoable. Reopen Tutorial from the menu at any time.

More tools

Pen draws freehand ink, and Highlighter draws wider translucent strokes. Halo surrounds your mouse with a ring. Spotlight darkens the screen outside the area around your mouse. Magnifying Glass enlarges live screen details. Halo, Spotlight, and Magnifying Glass let normal clicks reach your app.

Magnifying Glass needs Screen Recording permission for display content. Open Settings → Tools → Magnifying Glass → Permission Settings to see whether access is enabled and to open the macOS Screen Recording settings. If you grant access while kokofocus is open, select Magnifying Glass again to retry capture. Tutorial also links to the permission setting. Other display tools do not need this permission. Window Share authorizes its selected source through Apple’s picker. Frames stay in memory and are not recorded or uploaded by kokofocus.

Settings

General brings together the disk’s tools and background. Choose Light, Dark, Liquid Glass, or Transparent. It also controls the menu gesture, double-tap shortcut, hold length, and automatic update checks. Turn on Show circular menu in screen shares to let viewers see the menu while it is open, along with brief tool-change notices, in full-display shares and the kokofocus presentation window.

Tools lists every available tool. Select one to adjust its appearance and try the live preview. Move the mouse within a cursor-tool preview; hold the left mouse button and drag to test ink. Depending on the tool, controls include color, size, width, opacity, fill, fading, and magnification. Pointer and Laser each have a separate Show click ripple setting. Dependent settings are dimmed until enabled. Click an information icon to read about a setting.

Color controls offer five built-in colors and your saved custom colors. Use the standard color picker, give the color a name, then save it. Cancel closes the editor without saving. You can delete custom colors you no longer need. Rectangle and Ellipse use Shift for perfect squares and circles by default; choose a different modifier in their tool settings. Pointer settings also link to macOS’s native pointer controls.

Shortcuts lets you customize every tool and Undo, Redo, and Clear. Defaults are Control-P (Pointer), Control-L (Laser), Control-A (Arrow), Control-I (Line), Control-R (Rectangle), Control-E (Ellipse), Control-D (Pen), Control-H (Highlighter), Control-M (Magnifying Glass), Control-O (Halo), and Control-S (Spotlight). Action defaults are Control-Z (Undo), Control-Shift-Z (Redo), and Control-Delete (Clear).

Shortcuts work while kokofocus is on and pause while Settings is focused, Tutorial is open, or sharing setup is active. They preserve annotations when switching tools. Existing customized or cleared assignments survive upgrades. New defaults are added where available; conflicts use Control-Shift. A previous Clear All remains cleared.

Use × beside a shortcut to clear it, or Reset to Default Settings to restore shortcut defaults without changing other settings. Assignments without Command or Control show a typing-conflict warning; combinations macOS cannot register remain saved with an availability warning.

In version 1.2.3 and earlier, appearance controls are in the Appearance tab, the disk has the original six tools, and Laser passes clicks to the underlying app.

Sharing your screen

Share your whole display and your annotations appear automatically. Nothing else to configure.

Annotate a Single Window (beta)

Choose Microsoft Teams, Webex, or Zoom for a guide tailored to your meeting app. Each uses short instructions and an animation. Finish the step on screen, then click the button to continue. Click Cancel to close the guide.

Webex

  1. Stop sharing first. End any active screen or window share in Webex. In kokofocus’s menu, choose Annotate a Single Window (beta) → Webex.
  2. Prepare Webex. Open Settings → Sharing Content and turn on Use the Mac system screen sharing picker. The information icon in kokofocus explains why this is needed and offers optional motion guidance.
  3. If you changed the picker setting, restart Webex. Fully quit and reopen Webex, then rejoin your meeting.
  4. Choose your app window in kokofocus. Click Choose a Window…, then select the original app window you want to annotate in the macOS picker.
  5. Share that same window in Webex. Click Continue to Sharing, then click Share in Webex. When the Mac picker appears, click the same window on your desktop that you just chose in kokofocus.

Your pointer and drawings appear in the shared window. Keep working in your original app.

Microsoft Teams and Zoom

  1. Stop sharing first. End any active screen or window share in your meeting app. In kokofocus’s menu, choose Annotate a Single Window (beta), then Microsoft Teams or Zoom.
  2. Choose your app window in kokofocus. Click Choose a Window…, then select the original app window in the macOS picker.
  3. Share the annotated window. Confirm macOS’s sharing offer when available. Otherwise, click Continue to Sharing, open Share in your meeting app, and select kokofocus — Share This Window.

While you’re sharing

When macOS reports that sharing has started, kokofocus returns you to the original app so you can annotate and present. If screen recording or another capture is already active, first finish starting the share in your meeting, then choose Laser or a drawing tool from kokofocus’s menu or use its shortcut. The guide reminds you of this extra step when overlapping capture is detected.

When macOS reports that capture has ended, kokofocus closes the window share and stays on with your selected tool. If screen recording continues after your meeting stops sharing, choose Stop Window Share (beta) from the menu. Use this command too if your meeting app does not report when sharing stops.

Single-window annotation includes video only. Annotations keep their size and position from the window’s top-left when you resize it; they do not follow scrolling or changes in page layout.

This feature remains in beta, and behavior can vary by meeting app. If you have trouble, sharing your whole display also includes your annotations.

The circular menu and brief tool-change notices are hidden from captures by default. Turn on Settings → General → Show circular menu in screen shares to include them in a full-display share or the kokofocus presentation window used by Teams and Zoom. Direct sharing of the selected app window in Webex does not include these controls.

Updates

Automatic checks are on by default. While kokofocus is on, it checks Apple’s catalog at the top of each hour, such as 10:00 and 11:00. At startup, when you turn it on, or after waking, it also checks if there has not been an attempt in the current hour. Checks pause while kokofocus is off and cannot run when the app is quit. Failed checks retry at the next hour. Turn automatic checks off in Settings → General, or use Check for Updates to check manually.

A newer compatible release opens the same window as Check for Updates, with release notes and an Update in App Store button. It opens automatically once per version, even across restarts, and remains available through the Update Available menu item. Notification permission is not needed. The App Store installs the update; kokofocus does not download or install it itself.

Common questions

I selected Rectangle and now I cannot click my slides

Drawing tools take the mouse so you can draw without holding a key. Press Esc once, or select Pointer, to return clicks to your app. The menu bar stays reachable.

Control-click stopped working

The menu modifier sends clicks to kokofocus while held. Choose another key in Settings → General → Hold to open the menu if you need Control-click. Fn has no click gesture to replace.

Double-tapping Control conflicts with another app

Turn off double-tap in Settings → General. Tool shortcuts can be changed individually in Settings → Shortcuts; the app warns about assignments that may interfere with typing.

Why can’t I click my slides with Laser?

In version 1.3, Laser hides the native pointer and uses clicks for ripples. Press Esc to return to Pointer, or hold the configured menu key to open the disk. Tool shortcuts also work. Versions before 1.3 keep the native pointer visible and pass Laser clicks through.

Can shapes disappear on their own?

Yes. Select the tool in Settings → Tools, enable automatic fading, and choose a duration from 2 to 30 seconds. Earlier versions keep this control in Settings → Appearance.

Does it need permissions or a connection?

No tool needs Accessibility or Input Monitoring permission. Magnifying Glass needs Screen Recording permission for display content. Window Share uses the macOS picker to authorize one source for the session. Update checks contact Apple’s public catalog; the update window does not require notification permission. No account or tracking is used. See the privacy policy.

Resetting kokofocus

Your preferences live in the app’s own sandbox container. To reset them without uninstalling, quit kokofocus and delete:

~/Library/Containers/com.shutas.kokofocus
